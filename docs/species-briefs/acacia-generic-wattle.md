## Stage 1 -- Botanical Brief

_Completed by: Australian Botanist_

### 1.1 Habit Summary

- **Growth form**: Single-stemmed to few-stemmed upright tree, occasionally multi-stemmed from base (especially A. pycnantha). All three species are evergreen phyllode-bearing wattles that develop a defined trunk and spreading crown at maturity.
- **Typical mature height**:
  - A. mearnsii: 6--15 m (commonly 8--12 m in Melbourne region)
  - A. melanoxylon: 8--20 m (commonly 10--15 m; can reach 30 m in wet gullies but this is outside the Melbourne bioregion norm)
  - A. pycnantha: 3--8 m (commonly 4--6 m)
  - **Template range for generic model: 4--15 m** (parameterised by species)
- **Typical spread/crown width**:
  - A. mearnsii: 4--8 m
  - A. melanoxylon: 5--10 m
  - A. pycnantha: 3--6 m
  - **Template range: 3--10 m**
- **Typical trunk DBH**:
  - A. mearnsii: 15--35 cm
  - A. melanoxylon: 20--60 cm
  - A. pycnantha: 10--25 cm
  - **Template range: 10--60 cm**

### 1.2 Silhouette Features

The generic "wattle" silhouette is an upright tree with a rounded to broadly domed crown, lighter and more open than a eucalypt of equivalent height. Key distinguishing features at 5--15 m distance:

- **Crown shape**: Broadly rounded to dome-shaped. A. mearnsii tends toward a slightly irregular, open dome. A. melanoxylon develops the densest, most symmetrical dome. A. pycnantha has the widest, most spreading crown relative to its height -- often wider than tall at maturity.
- **Crown-to-trunk ratio**: The crown typically occupies 55--70% of total height. The trunk is relatively short and the canopy starts low compared to eucalypts.
- **Overall density**: Medium. The canopy reads as a solid mass of fine-textured foliage from distance, but is not as heavy or dark as Pittosporum or Coprosma. The fine phyllodes create a soft, feathery texture.
- **Distinguishing from eucalypts**: Wattles lack the pendulous, sparse quality of eucalypt canopies. The crown edge is more rounded and continuous rather than ragged. Foliage colour is a brighter, more yellow-green (not the grey-green of most eucalypts).
- **Trunk character in silhouette**: Generally straight to slightly sinuous, without the heavy lean or dramatic curves typical of River Red Gum. Bark is dark, giving the trunk a solid, dark profile.

**Species-specific silhouette variations:**
- A. mearnsii: More open, irregular crown; thinner canopy lets light through; often has a slight lean.
- A. melanoxylon: Densest crown of the three; most symmetrical; strong central leader gives a more columnar-to-dome shape in younger trees.
- A. pycnantha: Widest crown relative to height; branches often sweep low and outward; can appear almost shrubby in profile.

### 1.3 Bark Character

- **Type**: Smooth to finely fissured on young trees, becoming hard and furrowed with age. Not a stringybark, box, or ironbark type -- best described as "hard fissured" or "rough" bark.
- **Colour**:
  - A. mearnsii: Dark brown to almost black; this is the origin of the name "Black Wattle." Very dark trunk is a strong visual identifier.
  - A. melanoxylon: Dark grey-brown to blackish; deeply furrowed on older trees.
  - A. pycnantha: Grey-brown to dark grey; somewhat smoother than the other two, with shallower fissures.
  - **Template base colour: dark grey-brown to black-brown** (darken parameter for mearnsii, lighten slightly for pycnantha).
- **Texture notes**: Bark is persistent (does not shed in strips or plates like eucalypts). Fissures are longitudinal, running vertically up the trunk, 5--15 mm deep on mature trees. On young branches (< 5 cm diameter), bark is smooth and grey-green to brown. The transition from smooth branch bark to rough trunk bark occurs at roughly 8--15 cm diameter. A. melanoxylon develops the deepest fissures; A. pycnantha the shallowest.

### 1.4 Leaf Geometry

All three species bear phyllodes (flattened leaf-stalks functioning as leaves) at maturity. True bipinnate leaves are present only on juvenile plants and seedlings.

- **Shape**:
  - A. mearnsii: **Bipinnate compound** (uniquely among these three, adult A. mearnsii retains bipinnate leaves rather than transitioning fully to phyllodes). Each leaf has 8--25 pairs of pinnae, each pinna bearing 15--70 pairs of tiny pinnules. Individual pinnules are 1.5--3 mm long, 0.5--0.75 mm wide. Total compound leaf length: 50--120 mm.
  - A. melanoxylon: **Phyllode** -- narrowly elliptic to oblanceolate, slightly falcate (curved), with 3--5 prominent longitudinal veins. Phyllode length: 60--130 mm, width: 8--25 mm.
  - A. pycnantha: **Phyllode** -- broadly falcate (sickle-shaped), leathery, with a single prominent midvein and fine reticulate venation. Phyllode length: 80--200 mm, width: 15--50 mm. The largest and most visually prominent foliage of the three.
  - **Template approach**: The generic model should use a phyllode-based leaf. A. mearnsii's bipinnate foliage is a significant departure and would need a separate leaf texture/mesh, or a finely divided billboard texture that reads as feathery compound foliage.
- **Size summary for template parameterisation**:
  - Small/fine: A. mearnsii compound leaf (reads as a soft, fern-like mass)
  - Medium: A. melanoxylon phyllode (60--130 x 8--25 mm)
  - Large: A. pycnantha phyllode (80--200 x 15--50 mm)
- **Arrangement**: Alternate, spirally arranged along stems.
- **Petiole**: Phyllodes are essentially sessile (no distinct petiole -- the phyllode IS the modified petiole). A. mearnsii compound leaves have a rachis 3--8 mm long before the first pinna pair, with a raised gland on the rachis between the lowest pair.
- **Colour**:
  - Upper surface: Mid-green to bright green (A. pycnantha is the most vivid green; A. melanoxylon is slightly darker; A. mearnsii foliage has a soft grey-green cast due to the fine pinnules).
  - Lower surface: Slightly paler in all species.
  - **Overall canopy colour reads as bright mid-green to yellow-green**, distinctly warmer and brighter than co-occurring eucalypts.
- **Dimorphism**: All Acacia species produce true bipinnate compound leaves as juveniles. A. melanoxylon and A. pycnantha transition to phyllodes between 6--18 months of age. A. mearnsii retains bipinnate leaves throughout life (the only one of the three to do so). For the mature summer diorama, model adult foliage only.

### 1.5 Branching Habit

- **Primary branch angle**:
  - A. mearnsii: 40--60 degrees off trunk
  - A. melanoxylon: 30--50 degrees off trunk (more ascending)
  - A. pycnantha: 50--70 degrees off trunk (more spreading)
  - **Template range: 30--70 degrees** (parameterised)
- **Habit**:
  - A. mearnsii: Ascending to spreading; branches moderately arching.
  - A. melanoxylon: Ascending; strong apical dominance maintains a central leader; branches curve upward.
  - A. pycnantha: Spreading to slightly drooping at branch tips; the widest habit of the three; lower branches may sweep near horizontal.
  - **Template: ascending to spreading** (droop parameter increases for pycnantha)
- **Density**:
  - A. mearnsii: Moderate -- the fine bipinnate foliage creates visual density but branch framework itself is relatively open.
  - A. melanoxylon: Dense -- the most heavily branched of the three; develops a thick canopy.
  - A. pycnantha: Moderate to sparse -- fewer, thicker branches carrying large phyllodes.
  - **Template: moderate** (density parameter adjustable)
- **Distinctive patterns**: All three species can produce epicormic growth after damage or fire. A. pycnantha commonly produces basal suckers forming multi-stemmed clumps. A. mearnsii is a vigorous coloniser and often occurs in dense, even-aged stands. None exhibit the pendulous branchlet habit seen in eucalypts -- wattle branches are stiffer and more structured.

### 1.6 Seasonal Notes (Summer)

- **Flowering**:
  - A. mearnsii: Spent by mid-summer (flowers late spring to early summer, November--December). Some dried flower remnants may persist.
  - A. melanoxylon: Spent (flowers late winter to spring, August--October). No visible flowers in summer.
  - A. pycnantha: Spent (flowers late winter to early spring, August--October). The iconic golden balls are long gone by summer.
  - **In the summer diorama, none of the three species will be in flower.**
- **Leaf condition**: Full canopy. All three are evergreen and carry their maximum foliage load in summer. Canopies should be depicted at full density. New growth flushes are typically complete by early summer; foliage is mature and uniformly coloured by mid-summer.
- **Fruiting**:
  - A. mearnsii: Pods present, brown, 50--100 mm long, 5--8 mm wide, constricted between seeds, hanging in clusters. Visually noticeable.
  - A. melanoxylon: Pods present, brown to dark brown, 70--120 mm long, 8--12 mm wide, twisted and coiled when open, exposing seeds on orange-red funicles (arils). Distinctive coiled pods are a strong visual feature in summer.
  - A. pycnantha: Pods present, brown, 50--120 mm long, 5--8 mm wide, straight to slightly curved, often still green in early summer, browning by late summer.
  - **All three species carry visible seed pods in summer. This is the most relevant reproductive feature for the diorama.**
- **Other**: No notable bark shedding, no deciduous behaviour. Trees appear at their most vigorous and full in summer.

### 1.7 Co-occurring Species

- Canopy layer above: Eucalyptus camaldulensis (River Red Gum), Eucalyptus viminalis (Ribbon Gum)
- Ground layer below: Arthropodium strictum (Chocolate Lily), Dianella revoluta (Black-anther Flax-lily), Lomandra longifolia (Spiny-headed Mat-rush), Pteridium esculentum (Austral Bracken)

**River Banks and Creeklines** (where A. mearnsii and A. melanoxylon are prominent):
- Same layer: Acacia dealbata (Silver Wattle), Callistemon sieberi (River Bottlebrush), Exocarpos cupressiformis (Cherry Ballart), Melicytus dentatus (Tree Violet), Goodenia ovata (Hop Goodenia)
- Canopy layer above: Eucalyptus camaldulensis (River Red Gum)
- Ground layer below: Carex appressa (Tall Sedge), Carex fascicularis (Tassel Sedge), Lomandra longifolia (Spiny-headed Mat-rush), Microlaena stipoides (Weeping Grass)

**Grasslands and Woodlands on Fertile Plains** (present, not prominent):
- Wattles occur as scattered understorey trees beneath Eucalyptus camaldulensis and E. viminalis in this vegetation type.

**Relative scale note for the PlantFactory Specialist**: In the understorey layer, wattles typically reach 30--60% of the height of the co-occurring eucalypt canopy. A mature A. melanoxylon at 12 m will sit beneath a River Red Gum at 20--25 m. A. pycnantha at 5 m will be roughly the same height as Allocasuarina verticillata and Bursaria spinosa.

## Stage 2 -- Architectural Model

_Completed by: Plant Biologist_

### 2.1 Halle-Oldeman Model

- **Primary model**: Rauh's model
- **Confidence**: High
- **Secondary model**: Troll's model (for A. pycnantha in its spreading, multi-stemmed form)

### 2.2 Rationale

Rauh's model is defined by: (1) a monopodial trunk with rhythmic, indeterminate growth; (2) lateral branches that are morphologically equivalent to the trunk (also monopodial and indeterminate); (3) branches arising in acrotonic sequence with spiral phyllotaxis. All three Acacia species in this template conform to this pattern:

- **Monopodial trunk growth**: The Botanical Brief (Section 1.5) describes A. melanoxylon as having "strong apical dominance" maintaining "a central leader." A. mearnsii and A. pycnantha likewise develop a defined trunk, though apical dominance weakens earlier in A. pycnantha.
- **Indeterminate lateral branches**: Branches in all three species continue extending and producing sub-branches over multiple growing seasons. There is no terminal flowering that forces sympodial relay -- Acacia inflorescences are axillary (arising from phyllode axils), not terminal, so they do not terminate the shoot apex.
- **Rhythmic growth**: All three species produce discrete growth flushes visible as internode-length variation along shoots, consistent with Rauh's requirement for rhythmic rather than continuous growth.
- **Spiral phyllotaxis**: Section 1.4 confirms alternate, spirally arranged phyllodes/leaves.

The secondary consideration of Troll's model applies specifically to A. pycnantha when it grows as a multi-stemmed shrub-tree with weakened apical dominance and plagiotropic (horizontally inclined) main branches. However, even in A. pycnantha the trunk axes themselves remain monopodial, and multi-stemmed habit arises from basal sprouting rather than from sympodial relay. Rauh's model remains the correct primary assignment for the template.

### 2.3 Branching Type

- **Type**: Monopodial at all axes. The trunk is a monopodial main axis (A0). All lateral branch orders (A1 through A4) are also monopodial, with each branch maintaining its own apical meristem indefinitely.
- **Trunk axes**: Single-stemmed (A. mearnsii, A. melanoxylon) to multi-stemmed from the base (A. pycnantha). Multi-stemming in A. pycnantha arises from basal (epicormic/lignotuberous) sprouting, not from sympodial branching -- each basal stem is itself a monopodial axis.
- **Growth determinacy**: Indeterminate at all branching levels. No terminal inflorescences; all shoot apices remain vegetative.
- **Reiteration**: Present, age-dependent. Mature trees (especially A. melanoxylon and A. mearnsii) develop reiterated branch complexes -- partial copies of the whole-tree architecture arising on larger branches, contributing to crown thickening. Epicormic reiteration occurs after fire or damage in all three species. For the PlantFactory template, reiteration is modelled implicitly by allowing branching depth to reach Level 4.

### 2.4 Branching Hierarchy (Node Graph Specification)

This section defines the exact branching levels that map directly to Segment nodes in the PlantFactory node graph. Each level is a distinct Segment node.

**Level 0 -- Trunk (A0)**
- **Segment count**: 8--12 segments (each segment represents one growth unit along the trunk)
- **Internode length per segment**: 200--400 mm (longer in lower trunk, shortening acropetally toward the crown)
- **Total trunk height to first major fork / crown base**:
  - A. mearnsii: 2.5--5.0 m
  - A. melanoxylon: 3.0--6.0 m
  - A. pycnantha: 1.0--2.5 m
- **Taper**: Trunk radius decreases approximately linearly from base to crown base. Base radius = DBH/2 (see species values in Parameter Summary). Crown-base radius = 35--45% of base radius.
- **Gravitropism**: Strongly orthotropic (vertical). Slight lean (0--8 degrees off vertical) acceptable; increase lean range to 0--12 degrees for A. mearnsii.
- **Branching from this level**: Produces Level 1 branches via a Repeat node iterating once per segment, starting from segment 3--4 (i.e., the lower trunk is bare).

**Level 1 -- Primary scaffold branches (A1)**
- **Number per tree**: 5--12 primary branches (species-dependent: A. melanoxylon 8--12, A. mearnsii 6--10, A. pycnantha 5--8)
- **Insertion angle off trunk**:
  - A. mearnsii: 40--60 degrees
  - A. melanoxylon: 30--50 degrees
  - A. pycnantha: 50--70 degrees
- **Segment count per branch**: 5--8 segments
- **Internode length per segment**: 150--300 mm
- **Length (total)**: 1.0--3.0 m (species-scaled; A. melanoxylon longest, A. pycnantha shortest)
- **Taper**: Branch radius at origin = 25--40% of trunk radius at insertion point. Tapers to 20--30% of origin radius at tip.
- **Curvature/gravity response**: Branches curve upward (negative gravitropism) in A. melanoxylon (ascending habit); roughly plagiotropic (horizontal) in A. mearnsii; gently drooping at tips in A. pycnantha. Implement as a gravity parameter: A. melanoxylon = -0.2 (upward curve), A. mearnsii = 0.0 (neutral), A. pycnantha = +0.15 (slight droop).
- **Phyllotaxis of branch insertion on trunk**: Spiral, divergence angle 137.5 degrees (the Fibonacci angle). This is the standard for spiral phyllotaxis in dicots and applies here with high confidence.

**Level 2 -- Secondary branches (A2)**
- **Number per Level 1 branch**: 4--8 secondary branches
- **Insertion angle off parent**: 35--55 degrees (narrower range than Level 1; sub-branches tend toward 45 degrees)
- **Segment count per branch**: 4--6 segments
- **Internode length per segment**: 80--180 mm
- **Length (total)**: 0.4--1.2 m
- **Taper**: Origin radius = 30--45% of parent branch radius at insertion. Tapers to tip at 20--30% of origin.
- **Curvature/gravity**: Same gravity parameters as Level 1 but with greater magnitude (tips droop more under foliage weight). Add +0.05 to the Level 1 gravity value.
- **Phyllotaxis of insertion on parent**: Spiral, 137.5 degrees divergence.

**Level 3 -- Tertiary branchlets (A3)**
- **Number per Level 2 branch**: 3--6 branchlets
- **Insertion angle off parent**: 30--50 degrees
- **Segment count per branch**: 3--4 segments
- **Internode length per segment**: 40--100 mm
- **Length (total)**: 150--400 mm
- **Taper**: Origin radius = 30--40% of parent. Tapers to 2--3 mm tip diameter.
- **Curvature/gravity**: Gravity parameter same as Level 2 + 0.05 additional droop.
- **This is the primary foliage-bearing level.** Phyllodes/leaves are inserted along Level 3 branchlets.
- **Phyllotaxis of insertion**: Spiral, 137.5 degrees.

**Level 4 -- Terminal twigs (A4) [A. melanoxylon and A. mearnsii only]**
- **Number per Level 3 branchlet**: 2--4 twigs
- **Insertion angle**: 25--45 degrees
- **Segment count**: 2--3 segments
- **Internode length per segment**: 20--60 mm
- **Length (total)**: 50--150 mm
- **Foliage**: Dense leaf clusters on these terminal twigs.
- **A. pycnantha typically omits this level** because its larger phyllodes are borne directly on Level 3 branchlets, and its overall crown is less densely branched. For A. pycnantha, set Level 4 Repeat iterations to 0 or disable the Level 4 Segment node.

### 2.5 Phyllotaxis Specification

- **Pattern**: Spiral (2/5 phyllotaxis)
- **Divergence angle**: 137.5 degrees (the golden angle). This applies to:
  - Insertion of Level 1 branches on the trunk
  - Insertion of Level 2 branches on Level 1
  - Insertion of Level 3 branchlets on Level 2
  - Insertion of phyllodes/leaves on the foliage-bearing axis
- **Confidence**: High. Spiral phyllotaxis at 137.5 degrees is well-documented for Acacia and is the standard 2/5 arrangement observed in most Fabaceae. The Botanical Brief confirms "alternate, spirally arranged" leaves (Section 1.4).
- **Leaf spacing along foliage-bearing axes**:
  - A. mearnsii: Phyllode-equivalents (compound leaves) spaced 10--20 mm apart along Level 3/4 axes
  - A. melanoxylon: Phyllodes spaced 8--15 mm apart
  - A. pycnantha: Phyllodes spaced 15--30 mm apart (larger phyllodes, wider spacing)

### 2.6 Ontogenetic Notes

**Juvenile phase (0--2 years, not modelled):**
All three species produce bipinnate compound leaves as juveniles. A. melanoxylon and A. pycnantha undergo heteroblasty -- a transition from bipinnate leaves to phyllodes -- at 6--18 months. During this transition, intermediate forms (partially flattened petioles with reduced lamina) may appear. A. mearnsii retains bipinnate leaves throughout life. The juvenile phase is not relevant for the summer diorama mature tree model.

**Young tree phase (2--10 years):**
Architecture is strongly Rauh's model with clear apical dominance. The trunk is excurrent (a single dominant leader) in all three species. Crown is narrow and conical to elliptical. Branching is limited to Levels 0--2. This phase could be used for a "young" variant if needed.

**Mature phase (10+ years, target for model):**
Apical dominance weakens progressively. The crown broadens into the domed or spreading forms described in the Botanical Brief. The transition from excurrent (conical) to decurrent (domed) crown occurs at roughly:
- A. mearnsii: 8--12 years
- A. melanoxylon: 12--18 years (retains apical dominance longest)
- A. pycnantha: 5--8 years (loses apical dominance earliest)

At maturity the crown has fully developed to Level 3--4 branching depth. The trunk no longer extends significantly; height growth has essentially ceased. Crown expansion is lateral via extension of Level 1 and Level 2 branches.

**Critical transition for the model**: The mature tree must NOT look like a conifer (excurrent/conical). The apex of the trunk should terminate in a cluster of Level 1 branches that spread outward, creating the domed crown. Implement this by having the uppermost 2--3 Level 1 branches insert at shallower angles (closer to vertical, 15--30 degrees off trunk) so they form a crown apex that fans outward rather than continuing as a single spire.

### 2.7 Growth Unit Description

A single growth unit (flush) in Acacia consists of:
1. An extension of the existing shoot axis by 3--8 new internodes
2. Each new internode bears a single phyllode (or compound leaf in A. mearnsii) at its node
3. Axillary buds form in each phyllode axil; a subset will activate to produce the next-lower-order branches in subsequent growth flushes
4. The distal (newest) internodes of each flush are the shortest; internodes lengthen acropetally within a flush then shorten again at the tip -- producing a characteristic "longest in the middle" pattern within each growth unit

Growth flushes are seasonal. In Melbourne's climate, the primary flush occurs in spring (September--November), with a secondary smaller flush possible in autumn (March--April) if moisture is available. By mid-summer (the target diorama date), the current season's growth flush is complete and foliage is fully expanded.

The growth unit is visible on leafy branchlets as a slight constriction or colour change at the boundary between successive flushes. In the PlantFactory model, each Segment within a branching level approximates one growth unit.

### 2.8 Conflicts or Ambiguities

1. **A. pycnantha multi-stemmed habit**: The Botanical Brief notes A. pycnantha "commonly produces basal suckers forming multi-stemmed clumps." Rauh's model in its strict definition describes a single-trunked tree. Multi-stemming from the base does not violate Rauh's model -- each stem individually conforms to Rauh's -- but the PlantFactory template must accommodate this by optionally duplicating the Level 0 trunk axis 2--4 times with slight angular divergence (5--15 degrees off vertical for each stem) from a shared root base. This is a modelling consideration, not an architectural reclassification.

2. **A. mearnsii foliage representation**: A. mearnsii's bipinnate compound leaves are a fundamentally different geometry from the phyllodes of the other two species. Architecturally, the branching framework is identical -- the leaf type does not change the Rauh's model assignment. However, the PlantFactory Specialist must use a distinct leaf mesh or billboard texture for A. mearnsii. The compound leaf should be treated as a single leaf unit at the foliage-insertion node, not as an additional branching level (the pinnae and pinnules are part of the leaf organ, not stems).

3. **Troll's model consideration for A. pycnantha**: In exposed, poor-soil sites where A. pycnantha grows as a broad, low, spreading shrub-tree with strongly plagiotropic branches, the architecture superficially resembles Troll's model (in which all axes are plagiotropic and the "trunk" is a composite formed by successive branch bases, not a true monopodial axis). However, even in these forms, the main stems of A. pycnantha are genuinely monopodial with orthotropic (upright) orientation at least in their proximal portion. The spreading habit results from gravitational bending under foliage load and weakened apical dominance, not from a fundamentally different meristematic programme. Rauh's model remains correct; the spreading form is achieved by adjusting gravity and insertion-angle parameters rather than changing the branching logic.

4. **Crown apex modelling**: The transition from excurrent juvenile to decurrent mature form is a gradual ontogenetic process. The mature model should capture the final state only. There is a potential ambiguity in how to terminate the trunk apex -- in reality, the trunk simply ceases to dominate and the uppermost branches overtake it. For the node graph, the trunk Segment (Level 0) should simply end at its final segment, with the topmost Level 1 branches radiating outward from the terminal segments. No special sympodial relay or fork node is needed.

### 3.1 Node Graph Approach

- **Base template**: `template_rauh_acacia.tpf` (custom Rauh-model template built for this brief)
- **Rationale**: Stage 2 confirms Rauh's model at high confidence for all three species. ### 3.2 Template Node Graph Design

The template file `template_rauh_acacia.tpf` must be built manually in the PlantFactory GUI because Segment and flow-control nodes cannot be created via `AddNode()`.

```
Root (1)
 └── Trunk [Segment] (4)
      └── Primary [Segment] (5)
```

**Note on shared Leaf node**: PlantFactory allows a single Warpboard node to serve as the child of multiple Segment nodes. If this causes issues during template build, create two separate Warpboard nodes (indices 9 and 10), one parented to Tertiary and one to Twig, and adjust the index map accordingly. ### 3.6 Approved Simplifications

| Simplification | Botanical Reality | Implementation | Justification |
|---------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Single leaf mesh for all insertion points | Phyllodes vary in size along a branch (smaller near tip) | Uniform Warpboard size per species, no size gradient | Polygon budget; size variation not visible at diorama scale |
| No seed pods | All three species carry visible pods in summer | Omitted entirely | Pods are small relative to canopy; would add 2--5k tris per tree for minimal visual impact at 5--15m viewing distance |
| No multi-stem for A. pycnantha | A. pycnantha commonly forms multi-stemmed clumps | Single trunk only | Multi-stem would require duplicating the entire node graph or a separate template; can be faked at scene level by placing 2--3 instances with slight offset |
| Bipinnate leaf as billboard | A. mearnsii individual pinnules are 1.5--3mm | Alpha-masked billboard texture; no actual pinnule geometry | Modelling individual pinnules is impossible within polygon budget |
| No bark colour variation per species | Bark ranges from black-brown (mearnsii) to medium grey (pycnantha) | Single bark material; colour difference handled by template variant or post-processing in Blender | Simplifies template to one bark material |
| Level 4 disabled for A. pycnantha | Pycnantha does have some fine terminal branching | Set Level 4 child count to 0 | Large phyllodes on Level 3 provide sufficient crown density without Level 4 |
| No epicormic growth | Epicormic sprouts common after disturbance | Not modelled | The diorama depicts healthy undisturbed trees |
| Uniform spiral phyllotaxis | Real phyllotaxis has natural variation | Fixed 137.5-degree divergence at all levels | Variation is subtle and not perceptible at viewing distance |

  - Import FBX
  - Verify material assignments (bark + leaf)
  - Apply Draco compression on GLB export

- **Output format**: `.glb` (binary glTF with embedded textures)

```python
        )

    template_path = TEMPLATES[SPECIES]
    output_path = os.path.join(OUTPUT_DIR, spec["output_file"])

    # Ensure output directory exists
    os.makedirs(OUTPUT_DIR, exist_ok=True)

    print(f"=== Acacia {SPECIES} Generation ===")
    print(f"Template: {template_path}")
    print(f"Output:   {output_path}")

    # Get application handles
    app = eon.EONApplication()
    child = eon.EONChild()

    # Load template with species-specific seed
    print(f"Loading template with seed {spec['seed']}...")
    child.LoadPlant(template_path, spec["seed"])

    # Set global parameters
    child.SetAgeRatio(spec["age"])
    child.SetHealth(spec["health"])
    child.SetSeason(spec["season"])

        print(f"  Setting params for {node_name} (index {node_index})...")

        for param_name, param_value in node_params.items():
            child.NodeSetParam(node, param_name, param_value)

    # Apply all parameter changes
    print("Applying changes...")
    child.ApplyChanges()

    # Wait for geometry generation
    print("Waiting for geometry...")
    child.WaitForGeometry()

    # Check polygon budget
    poly_count = child.GetNumberOfPolygons()
    leaf_count = child.GetLeafNumber()
    plant_height = child.GetPlantHeight()
    print(f"  Polygons:    {poly_count}")
    print(f"  Leaves:      {leaf_count}")

    if poly_count > MAX_POLYGONS:
        print(f"  WARNING: Polygon count {poly_count} exceeds budget {MAX_POLYGONS}!")
        print(f"  Consider reducing Level 3 or Level 4 'Number' parameter.")
    else:
        print(f"  OK: Within polygon budget ({poly_count}/{MAX_POLYGONS})")

    # Configure export
    child.SetExportPreset(eon.EExportPreset_EP_Blender)
    child.SetMeshingMode(eon.GT_realtime, eon.SPM_Triangles)

    # Export FBX
    print(f"Exporting to {output_path}...")

    print("Export complete.")

    child.RenderToFile(thumb_path, "LRT|TRBG", 512, 512)
```

### 3.10 Template Build Instructions

These are step-by-step instructions for building `template_rauh_acacia.tpf` in the PlantFactory GUI. This template is then duplicated three times with different leaf materials for each species variant.

#### Prerequisites

- PlantFactory 2024 installed at `/Applications/PlantFactory/`
- Leaf textures prepared (see Section 3.5):
  - `tex_leaf_bipinnate_diffuse.png` + `tex_leaf_bipinnate_alpha.png` (512x512)
  - `tex_leaf_phyllode_narrow_diffuse.png` + `tex_leaf_phyllode_narrow_alpha.png` (512x512)
  - `tex_leaf_phyllode_broad_diffuse.png` + `tex_leaf_phyllode_broad_alpha.png` (512x512)
- Bark texture prepared:
  - `tex_bark_acacia_diffuse.png` + `tex_bark_acacia_normal.png` (1024x1024, tileable)

#### Step 1: Create New Scene

1. Open PlantFactory.
2. Go to **File > New** to create a fresh scene.
3. The default scene creates 4 nodes: Age (0), Root (1), Season (2), Health (3).
4. Verify by checking the Node Graph panel -- you should see these four nodes.

#### Step 2: Add Trunk Segment (Index 4)

1. In the Node Graph panel, right-click on empty space.
2. Select **Add Node > Geometry > Segment**.
3. A new Segment node appears. Rename it to **"Trunk"** by double-clicking its title.
4. Connect the Trunk Segment to the Root node: drag from Trunk's output to Root's input slot.
5. In the Trunk's parameter panel, set initial values:
   - Number: 11
   - Length: 10.0
   - Radius: 0.125
   - Branch start: 0.35
   - Branch end: 0.95
   - Axial subdivisions: 6
   - Radial subdivisions: 8

#### Step 3: Add Primary Branch Segment (Index 5)

1. Right-click in the Node Graph panel.
2. Select **Add Node > Geometry > Segment**.
3. Rename to **"Primary"**.
4. Connect Primary's output to Trunk's child input slot (the slot on the right side of the Trunk node labelled "children" or similar).
5. Set initial parameters:
   - Number: 8
   - Branch angle: 50.0
   - Branch rotation: 137.5
   - Length: 2.5
   - Radius: 0.04
   - Axial subdivisions: 4
   - Radial subdivisions: 5

#### Step 4: Add Secondary Branch Segment (Index 6)

1. Right-click > **Add Node > Geometry > Segment**.
2. Rename to **"Secondary"**.
3. Connect Secondary's output to Primary's child input.
4. Set initial parameters:
   - Number: 5
   - Branch angle: 45.0
   - Branch rotation: 137.5
   - Length: 0.8
   - Radius: 0.015
   - Axial subdivisions: 3
   - Radial subdivisions: 4

#### Step 5: Add Tertiary Branch Segment (Index 7)

1. Right-click > **Add Node > Geometry > Segment**.
2. Rename to **"Tertiary"**.
3. Connect Tertiary's output to Secondary's child input.
4. Set initial parameters:
   - Number: 4
   - Branch angle: 40.0
   - Branch rotation: 137.5
   - Length: 0.25
   - Radius: 0.005
   - Axial subdivisions: 2
   - Radial subdivisions: 3

#### Step 6: Add Twig Segment (Index 8)

1. Right-click > **Add Node > Geometry > Segment**.
2. Rename to **"Twig"**.
3. Connect Twig's output to Tertiary's child input (first child slot).
4. Set initial parameters:
   - Number: 3
   - Branch angle: 35.0
   - Branch rotation: 137.5
   - Length: 0.10
   - Radius: 0.003
   - Axial subdivisions: 2
   - Radial subdivisions: 3

#### Step 7: Add Leaf Warpboard (Index 9)

1. Right-click > **Add Node > Geometry > Warpboard**.
2. Rename to **"Leaf"**.
3. Connect Leaf's output to **both**:
   - Tertiary's child input (second child slot, alongside Twig), AND
   - Twig's child input
4. If PlantFactory does not support a single Warpboard connected to two parents, create two Warpboard nodes (Leaf_L3 at index 9 and Leaf_L4 at index 10) and connect one to each parent Segment. Update the node index map accordingly.
5. Set initial parameters:
   - Scale: 0.06
   - Radius: 0.5
   - Flexibility: 0.3

#### Step 8: Create Bark Material

1. Select the Trunk Segment node (or any Segment node -- bark material is typically shared).
2. In the Material panel, create a new material. Name it **"mat_bark_acacia"**.
3. Set material type to standard PBR.
4. Load `tex_bark_acacia_diffuse.png` into the Diffuse/Color channel.
5. Load `tex_bark_acacia_normal.png` into the Normal channel.
6. Set Roughness to 0.85.
7. Set Metallic to 0.0.
8. Apply this material to ALL Segment nodes (Trunk, Primary, Secondary, Tertiary, Twig).

#### Step 9: Create Leaf Material

1. Select the Leaf Warpboard node.
2. In the Material panel, create a new material. Name it **"mat_leaf_phyllode_narrow"** (for the melanoxylon base variant).
3. Set material type to **Two-Sided** (`MaterialType_TwoSided`).
4. Load `tex_leaf_phyllode_narrow_diffuse.png` into the Diffuse/Color channel.
5. Load `tex_leaf_phyllode_narrow_alpha.png` into the Alpha channel.
6. Set Backlight/Translucency to 0.25.
7. Enable alpha testing/transparency.

#### Step 10: Save Base Template

1. Go to **File > Save As**.
2. Save to `~/Documents/e-on software/PlantFactory/Species/Personal/template_rauh_acacia_melanoxylon.tpf`.

#### Step 11: Create Species Variants

1. **For A. mearnsii variant**:
   - With the template still open, select the Leaf Warpboard node.
   - Change its material to **"mat_leaf_bipinnate"**: swap diffuse and alpha textures to the bipinnate versions.
   - Save As: `template_rauh_acacia_mearnsii.tpf`.

2. **For A. pycnantha variant**:
   - Re-open the melanoxylon template.
   - Select the Leaf Warpboard node.
   - Change its material to **"mat_leaf_phyllode_broad"**: swap diffuse and alpha textures to the broad phyllode versions.
   - Save As: `template_rauh_acacia_pycnantha.tpf`.

#### Step 12: Verify Node Indices

After saving each template, run a verification script inside PlantFactory to confirm the node index map:

```python

child.LoadPlant("path/to/template.tpf", 0)

    try:
        node = child.GetNodeAt(i)
        print(f"Index {i}: {node}")
    except:
        print(f"Index {i}: (no node)")

```

Confirm that indices 0--3 are Age/Root/Season/Health and indices 4--9 match the node layout in Section 3.2. If the order differs from expected, update the `PARAMS` dictionary in the automation script to match actual indices.

### 3.11 Open Issues

| Issue | Status | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Segment node parameter names | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | The UI label names for Segment params (e.g. "Length", "Branch start", "Branch rotation") are inferred from `node_tooltips.xml` and PlantFactory documentation. They have NOT been confirmed via runtime `NodeSetParam()` calls on template-loaded Segment nodes. First template test must verify every parameter name and log failures. |
| Material switching via API | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | Can `NodeSetParam()` change the material assigned to a Warpboard node? If yes, a single template file could serve all three species. If no, the three-template-variant approach (Section 3.3) is required. |
| Shared Warpboard node | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | Can a single Warpboard node be connected as a child of two different Segment nodes simultaneously? If not, two separate Warpboard nodes are needed (one for Level 3, one for Level 4), changing the index map. |
| Node index stability | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | Does the node index order remain stable across saves and reloads? The automation script relies on fixed indices. Must verify after template construction. |
| Gravity / branch curvature | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | Profile filters on Branch angle (`NodeResetProfileFilter` + `NodeAddProfileFilterPoint`) are the planned mechanism for species-specific branch curvature. These API calls have not been tested on Segment nodes. If they fail, branch curvature must be set manually in each template variant. |
| Polygon count accuracy | `[NEEDS TESTING]` | The polygon budget estimates in Section 3.4 are calculated from parameter values. Actual counts from `GetNumberOfPolygons()` may differ. First successful generation must be checked and parameters adjusted if over budget. |
| Bark colour per species | `[NEEDS DISCUSSION]` | Current approach uses a single bark material for all three species. A. mearnsii's very dark (near-black) bark versus A. pycnantha's lighter grey bark may warrant separate bark materials or a colour-adjustable parameter. Could be handled in Blender post-processing instead. |
